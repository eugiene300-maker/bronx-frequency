A Bookish Afternoon in Mott Haven: The Lit. Bar

Books, conversation and a little time for yourself on Alexander Avenue.

By Bronx Frequency Editorial Desk
Published August 25, 2026 · Updated August 31, 2026 · 5 min read

At a glance

The Lit. Bar brings an independent bookstore and wine bar together at 131 Alexander Avenue.

A bookstore can be the whole plan. You don’t need a reading list, a literary occasion or a friend who finishes three novels a week. At The Lit. Bar in Mott Haven, the starting point is much simpler: make some time for books, see what catches your attention and let the afternoon take its own shape.

The independent bookstore and wine bar is at 131 Alexander Avenue. Founded by Bronx native Noëlle Santos, it opened in April 2019 and has become a recognizable part of the South Bronx’s literary life. Its combination of books and a social setting gives readers a place to browse without treating reading as an activity that has to happen alone.

For an easy first visit, build the day around the store rather than squeezing it between five other stops. Here’s how to make that work, whether you’re arriving with a friend, looking for a new book club choice or taking yourself out for a few quiet hours.

Start with what you actually like to read

There’s no need to arrive with the name of an impressive author. Think about the last book you stayed up to finish, the subject you keep bringing up at dinner or the kind of story you wish someone would tell you. Those are useful starting points for browsing.

If you tend to get overwhelmed by shelves of possibilities, give yourself one small mission. Look for a Bronx author, a short novel you could finish this month or a collection of essays about a subject you already care about. A little direction helps without turning a bookstore visit into homework.

You can also let the format make the choice. A poetry collection works differently from a long novel; a book of photographs invites a different kind of attention from a memoir. Pick something that suits the reading time you genuinely have, not an imaginary version of your schedule.

The story behind the store

Santos grew up in Soundview and studied at Lehman College before becoming a bookseller. The possibility of the Bronx losing its Barnes & Noble helped push her toward opening an independent bookstore of her own. Bringing the idea to life involved learning the business, testing it through pop-ups and gathering community support.

That background matters because The Lit. Bar isn’t simply a shop that happened to land in the Bronx. The borough is part of the reason it exists. Its attention to local and Black feminist authors gives the idea a tangible place on the shelves.

You don’t have to know the full business history to enjoy a visit. But it adds something to understand that the person behind the counter built a space in response to a local need. A recommendation from an independent bookseller can carry that sense of purpose alongside a very ordinary pleasure: helping someone find their next good read.

Make it a two-person outing

A bookstore is a useful place to meet someone when you want conversation without needing to talk every second. Start by browsing separately. Then meet back up and show each other one title you considered, one cover that caught your eye and one book you already know you love.

This works especially well with friends whose tastes don’t overlap. You might leave with a reading suggestion you would never have chosen for yourself. You might also discover that a person you’ve known for years has a whole interest you’ve barely discussed.

If you’re planning to stay for the wine-bar side of the visit, confirm the day’s arrangements and seating first. A bookstore visit doesn’t require ordering a drink. Make sure the plan works for the person who would rather browse, the person who wants to sit and the person who isn’t drinking.

Give a book club a better starting point

Choosing a book is often the part of a book club that takes the longest. Everybody has a suggestion, nobody wants to insist, and suddenly the conversation has produced another month without a selection.

Try a short shortlist instead. Bring two or three possible titles, agree on a realistic page count and decide when everyone would be able to start. Ask about availability before committing a whole group to the same book. A title that suits one reader’s budget or preferred format may not suit everyone else.

An initial visit can be about choosing, rather than holding a full discussion in the shop. If you want to organize a gathering there, ask about space and event arrangements instead of assuming a group can take over a table. Small planning choices help keep the place comfortable for other readers, too.

Shopping for someone else

A book can be a thoughtful gift without being an attempt to improve the recipient. Start with what they enjoy, not what you think they should study. A person who loves neighborhood history may welcome a photo book; someone with a long commute may prefer a paperback they can carry easily.

If you’re asking for help, offer a few clues: the recipient’s interests, an author they’ve liked and the kind of reading mood you have in mind. That gives a bookseller more to work with than “something good.”

For a personal touch, add a note explaining why you chose it. You don’t have to predict that the book will change their life. “This reminded me of our conversation” is often enough.

Keep the rest of Mott Haven in proportion

Alexander Avenue is the anchor for this outing. Once you’ve settled on a visit time, decide whether you want food before or afterward and choose a nearby stop that is actually open then. Don’t turn a relaxed bookstore afternoon into a cross-borough sprint.

If you have only an hour, spend it at the store. If you have longer, leave some of the time unassigned. A good local plan gives you permission to stay with the thing you’re enjoying rather than constantly move on to the next recommendation.

People interested in cannabis culture often share an interest in music, art and independent businesses. That can be part of the magazine’s audience without making every place a consumption venue. The Lit. Bar belongs in a Bronx cultural guide because of its books, founder and community role.

Before you head over

Use 131 Alexander Avenue as your destination, and confirm current opening hours and any author-event arrangements before traveling. A special reading may require a reservation even when ordinary browsing does not. If seating or step-free access is important to your visit, ask about the specific area you plan to use.

Bring a bag if you expect to leave with books, allow yourself time to browse and keep your phone out of the decision for a little while. The best purchase may be the title you didn’t know existed when you walked in.

And if you leave with nothing this time, you’ve still learned something about your own reading taste. There’s always another shelf to explore on the next visit.
