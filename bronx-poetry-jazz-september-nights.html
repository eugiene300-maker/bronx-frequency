Poetry, Jazz and Art: September Nights in the Bronx

A David Mills reading, a Langston Hughes-inspired evening and a new exhibition to put on your calendar.

By Bronx Frequency Editorial Desk
Published August 29, 2026 · Updated August 31, 2026 · 5 min read

At a glance

David Mills reads at Bartow-Pell on September 24. A jazz and Langston Hughes program is listed at BRAC for September 25.

There’s more than one way to spend a Bronx evening with music and words. You can sit close enough to hear the pauses in a poem, catch a live performance or make time for an exhibition before dinner. This September brings a few particularly interesting places to start, including Bartow-Pell Mansion Museum and the Bronx River Art Center.

The appeal is the scale. These are outings you can build around one program rather than a whole evening of travel and reservations. They also put different corners of the borough in the picture: a historic house in Pelham Bay Park and an arts center on East Tremont Avenue.

Here are the dates and the practical details, along with a few ways to make the most of a night that asks you to listen closely.

September 24: David Mills at Bartow-Pell

Writer and actor David Mills is scheduled to read at Bartow-Pell on Thursday, September 24, from 7 to 8:30 p.m. The program, Expanding the Discourse, takes place in the double parlors and draws on his poetry about slavery in colonial-era New York.

The featured books are Unhired Hands, Boneyarn and How the Earth Answers. Mills was a 2025–2026 New York Public Library Cullman Center Fellow. You don’t have to arrive having read all three collections to follow the evening; the reading itself offers a way into the work.

A historic-house setting gives this kind of program an additional layer. The words aren’t floating in a neutral room. You’re listening in a place already associated with questions about the past, the people who lived there and the people whose labor supported it.

How to approach a poetry reading

If poetry isn’t usually your first choice, don’t worry about understanding every line as it happens. Listen for a phrase, an image or a question that stays with you. A live reading brings rhythm and emphasis that can be less obvious on a first encounter with a printed page.

Resist the urge to turn the whole event into note-taking. Write down a title or a thought if it helps, then return to listening. You can explore a book afterward without trying to recreate the entire performance in the moment.

If there’s an audience question period, keep your question brief enough to leave room for the answer. A specific question about an image or a writing choice often opens a better conversation than a long statement about your own experience with the subject.

September 25: a jazz-and-Langston lead

The Bronx arts calendar also lists Midnight, Jazz & Langston: Navigating Kindred Spirits at Bronx River Art Center on Friday, September 25, at 6:30 p.m. The center is at 1087 East Tremont Avenue.

This is a date to confirm directly with the organizer before making a firm plan. Event details, admission arrangements and running times can change, and the title alone doesn’t tell you the format of the evening. Ask whether it is a performance, discussion or a combination, and whether reservations are required.

The pairing of jazz and Langston Hughes gives the program a clear literary and musical starting point. It also makes a natural companion to the Bartow-Pell reading, even if you choose only one of the two nights.

Make room for visual art, too

Bronx River Art Center’s September exhibition, Internal Anchor, is curated by Fable Jones and runs from September 10 through October 10, 2026. That gives visitors another reason to keep the center in mind beyond a single evening event.

Gallery access and event admission are separate questions. Confirm the gallery hours for the date you choose rather than assuming an evening program includes time in the exhibition. The center’s ordinary office hours aren’t the same thing as its exhibition hours, either.

If the timing works, seeing art before a performance can make the visit feel fuller without requiring another journey. Leave enough space between the two to eat, settle in and switch your attention from looking to listening.

Two venues, two different journeys

Bartow-Pell is in Pelham Bay Park. Bronx River Art Center is on East Tremont Avenue near West Farms Square. They are not interchangeable stops, and this guide isn’t suggesting that you rush between them on the same night.

Choose the program first, then plan the route around its actual location and ending time. If you’re meeting a friend, share the full venue name. “The arts place in the Bronx” is an easy way for two people to end up in different neighborhoods.

For a late return, check the trip home before you leave. That includes the final stretch from the venue to your stop, any accessibility requirements and what you’ll do if a service change makes the ordinary route less convenient.

A good outing for going alone

A reading or small performance is a comfortable solo plan because the main activity is already shared. You don’t need to fill a table or maintain a conversation all evening. Arrive, find your place and let the program give the night its shape.

If you feel like talking afterward, start with the thing everyone has just experienced. A simple question about a favorite passage or moment can be enough. If you’d rather leave quietly, that’s equally fine.

Solo visits can also make it easier to try something outside your usual interests. You can choose the poetry reading without having to persuade a group, or spend longer with an exhibition without feeling that you’re holding anyone up.

Keep the plan affordable and comfortable

Before committing, find out the full admission cost and whether the program requires a separate reservation. Don’t assume that a venue’s free exhibition policy applies to every special event. Likewise, don’t assume an event is expensive just because it takes place in an impressive building.

Eat at a time that lets you arrive without rushing. If you need a particular seat or an accessible entrance, ask ahead about the room being used. A historic building may have different arrangements for different spaces.

Bring only what you want to carry through the event. A small bag, a notebook if you enjoy using one and a charged phone for the return trip are more useful than treating the evening like a full day out.

Take one thing home with you

It might be a book title, a line you want to think about or the name of a venue you’d like to visit again. That is enough. A cultural night out doesn’t have to leave you with a complete new area of expertise.

September 24 and 25 offer two starting points, and the Internal Anchor exhibition provides a longer window. Pick the one that fits your curiosity and your schedule. The borough’s creative life becomes easier to find when you begin with one real date and one place you’re willing to get to know.
