Three Fall Shows to Put on Your Lehman Center Calendar

Salsa, merengue and R&B are on the Bronx lineup.

By Bronx Frequency Editorial Desk
Published August 22, 2026 · Updated August 31, 2026 · 5 min read

At a glance

Save September 26, October 17 and November 7 for three different nights of live music.

A night of live music doesn’t have to start with a trip out of the Bronx. Lehman Center’s fall lineup brings salsa, merengue and R&B into the mix, with three dates worth putting on the calendar.

Choose the night by the music you want

The three dates in this guide offer different musical starting points. Grupo Galé and Sonora Carruseles bring a salsa focus to September 26. Milly Quezada and Miriam Cruz give October 17 a merengue connection. Ginuwine and Ro James make November 7 an R&B option.

That variety is useful when you’re organizing an outing with people who don’t all follow the same artists. Ask which night actually interests them rather than trying to sell everybody on a generic concert plan.

You can also choose according to the company. One show may be a good reason to bring a relative who knows the music well; another may suit a friend you’ve been meaning to catch up with. The right evening is the one people are excited to attend, not simply the first open date.

A concert can be a whole evening without being a complicated one

Start with the performance time and build only what you need around it. Decide whether to eat beforehand, how you’ll get there and where you’ll meet. Those three decisions often do more for the night than adding several extra stops.

If dinner is part of the plan, leave enough time that the meal doesn’t become a race against the curtain. If the group would rather keep costs down, eat separately and meet at the venue. Neither approach makes the outing less social.

Afterward, let people choose whether to continue the evening or head home. A concert can feel complete without requiring everyone to stay out for the same number of hours.

Buying tickets as a group

Before one person buys for everyone, agree on the budget and the seats. A vague “anything is fine” can become less fine when the final total includes costs nobody discussed.

Confirm the performance date, the ticket terms and the seating arrangement before completing a purchase. Make sure the whole group understands whether tickets can be transferred or changed. This is especially useful when people are planning several weeks ahead.

Keep the tickets organized and decide how each person will access theirs. If the group is arriving separately, avoid making everyone depend on the one person who has the tickets and is running late.

Give an unfamiliar artist a chance

You don’t need to know every song before attending a show. A live performance can be the introduction. If you want a little context, listen to a few tracks in advance and notice what you’re drawn to: the rhythm, the voice or the way the arrangement develops.

Avoid turning preparation into an assignment. The point is to make the evening more enjoyable, not to prove you deserve to be in the audience.

Going with someone who loves the artist can add another layer. Ask which song or part of the performer’s career matters to them. Their answer may tell you something about your friend as well as the music.

A night out across generations

Music can make a group outing work when ages and interests differ. A parent, an older relative and a younger friend may connect with the same performance for different reasons.

Talk about the practical needs without making them awkward. Some people want a shorter walk, a particular seat or an earlier meal. Others may be happy to adapt if they understand the plan.

Don’t assume that enthusiasm for the artist answers every comfort question. A person can be excited about a show and still need a manageable route, a clear ending plan or help with ticket access.

Arriving comfortably

Aim to reach the venue with enough time to find the entrance, use the facilities and locate your seat before the performance begins. A little space at the start makes the whole evening feel less rushed.

If accessibility arrangements matter, ask about the specific seats and route you’ll use. A general statement about a venue may not answer a question about a particular section or entrance.

Keep the meeting point simple and specific. For a group, choose one place everyone can identify. That reduces the chance of spending the opening minutes coordinating people who are standing on different sides of the building.

Be part of a good audience

Live music has a shared atmosphere, and small choices affect the people around you. Follow the venue’s rules on recording, keep a phone screen from becoming someone else’s brightest view and pay attention to what the performance invites.

Different shows have different energy. Some moments call for movement and response; others are easier to enjoy when the room listens. You don’t need to be stiff about it. Notice the setting and the people nearby.

If you need to step out, do so considerately and follow staff guidance about returning. The goal is to enjoy the night while helping everyone else do the same.

Keep the return trip as clear as the arrival

Check the journey home before leaving for the show. Late-evening service can differ from the trip you know during the day, and a group may be splitting toward several destinations.

If someone is driving, decide that arrangement ahead of time and keep it compatible with a safe trip home. Don’t leave the question until the show is over and everyone is tired.

A clear return plan makes it easier to relax during the performance. It also means the last memory of the evening can be the music rather than a long, avoidable scramble afterward.

Three dates, several ways to enjoy them

You could choose one show for a special occasion, make a small fall concert tradition or simply pick the artist you most want to hear. The lineup doesn’t need to become another list you feel obliged to complete.

Lehman Center gives Bronx listeners a local place to build those plans. Confirm the details for September 26, October 17 or November 7, choose your company and let the performance carry the evening.

The best concert plan often feels straightforward once it is settled: a real date, a seat you’re happy with and music you want to spend time hearing.

September 26: Grupo Galé and Sonora Carruseles

For the salsa fans in the group, this is the date to flag. Pick your company first, then confirm the performance details before making the rest of the evening’s plans.

October 17: Milly Quezada and Miriam Cruz

Two familiar names for merengue lovers share this October date. It’s a good reason to get that long-discussed group outing out of the conversation and onto a calendar.

November 7: Ginuwine and Ro James

The November lineup brings an R&B night to the venue. If your group has been waiting for a different musical mood, keep this one in mind.

Before the show

Confirm start times, ticket availability and any accessibility arrangements with the box office. Give yourself time to arrive without rushing. The point is to enjoy the night, not spend the first song finding your seat.
