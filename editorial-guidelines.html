Editorial guidelines

Clear sourcing, fair attribution and corrections readers can find.

These standards apply to the magazine, its local guide and its podcast.

Accuracy and evidence

Use primary sources for rules, official notices, event dates and institutional information whenever possible. Credit another newsroom’s original reporting. Separate what a source establishes from analysis and from questions that remain open.

Bylines and reporting labels

The Editorial Desk is an organizational credit. An individual byline must reflect that person’s actual work. A researched profile is not a firsthand review. An article must not imply an interview, visit, test or observation that did not occur.

Fairness

Check disputed claims and give people a fair opportunity to respond. Respect the privacy of residents who are not public figures.

Sponsorship

Disclose material financial relationships. Label sponsor links. A sponsor may identify a factual error, but a preference for favorable coverage is not evidence. No business receives an invented review, rating or testimonial.

Dates and updates

The header displays the current local date and time. Each article retains its actual publication date. A substantive update has its own date and, when needed, a correction note. Automated clock changes never refresh article dates or structured data.

The attraction guide

The guide describes places and their published rules. “No smoking or vaping” is a policy label, not a judgment about people. “Policy not confirmed” is not permission. Labels do not provide individualized legal advice or assert that every form of consumption is equivalent.

Health and public rules

Use authoritative information, make its scope clear and avoid individual diagnoses or promised legal outcomes. Direct readers to qualified help for personal medical or legal questions.

Photographs and illustrations

Photographs carry creator, source and license information. Older photographs are dated and are not presented as current event coverage. Editorial illustrations are identified as illustrations. Images do not substitute for reporting.

Corrections

A substantive correction should explain what changed and when. Changes to an event schedule are updates, not necessarily errors in an earlier dated account. Readers can use the contact page to send a precise correction note.
