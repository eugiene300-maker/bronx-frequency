An Arthur Avenue Afternoon, Starting at Mike’s Deli

Lunch, a market browse and something good to bring home from Belmont.

By Bronx Frequency Editorial Desk
Published August 28, 2026 · Updated August 31, 2026 · 5 min read

At a glance

Start at the Arthur Avenue Retail Market, 2344 Arthur Avenue, and leave time for Mike’s Deli and the surrounding counters.

An Arthur Avenue afternoon is best approached with an appetite and a little flexibility. The draw is the concentration: food businesses close enough together that you can compare, browse and build a meal without turning the outing into a long journey between neighborhoods.

Start at the Arthur Avenue Retail Market, at 2344 Arthur Avenue in the Bronx’s Little Italy. Mike’s Deli is one of its familiar food anchors, with Italian specialties that include cheeses, cured meats, pasta and sauces. The market also contains the Bronx Beer Hall, giving the indoor space a social side alongside the food shopping.

The useful question isn’t how many things you can eat in one visit. It’s what kind of afternoon you want. A quick lunch, a take-home dinner and an outing with friends can all start in the same place and turn into very different plans.

Begin with a look around

It is tempting to make the first decision as soon as you arrive hungry. Give yourself a few minutes to see the space instead. Notice which counters you want to return to, which items are suited to eating now and which are better taken home.

An indoor market rewards this small pause. You can work out what fits together before spending your whole budget at the first stop. It also helps when different people in the group want different things. Someone can be interested in a sandwich while someone else is thinking about ingredients for dinner.

Keep passageways clear while you decide. Step aside from an active counter if you need a longer discussion with friends. The market is a working place, and making room for people who know exactly what they came for is part of settling into its rhythm.

Make Mike’s Deli your starting point

Mike’s Deli is associated with Italian-American food traditions and family business history in the market. Cheeses, cured meats and prepared specialties give you several ways to approach it, depending on whether you want lunch or something to use later.

Ask about the item you’re considering rather than feeling that you need to know the whole range in advance. If it’s for a meal at home, say how you plan to serve it. If it needs to travel, ask about packing and refrigeration. Those details matter more than trying to order the thing that will look most impressive in a photograph.

A food outing can be especially satisfying when part of it follows you home. A sauce or a piece of cheese can turn into a second meal, giving the afternoon a little more life than a single lunch stop.

Choose an eat-now plan or a take-home plan

For an eat-now visit, keep purchases manageable and decide where you’ll eat before collecting several different items. Confirm seating arrangements rather than assuming every food purchase comes with a table. A crowded market can feel very different from a quiet one.

For a take-home visit, think about the rest of your route. Perishable food shouldn’t spend the afternoon accompanying you through unrelated errands. Bring an insulated bag when appropriate and ask the business about handling anything you’re unsure of.

You can combine the two plans, but put the take-home shopping near the end. Have lunch, browse a little, then collect the ingredients you want to carry back. It’s a small change that can make the whole outing simpler.

Build a dinner without buying everything

A market can make an ordinary dinner feel more considered. Choose one centerpiece and let the rest stay straightforward. Pasta and a sauce might be enough of a starting point; cheese and cured meats could become part of a meal you already know how to assemble.

Before buying, take a moment to remember what is at home. The goal is a dinner you’ll actually make, not a collection of appealing ingredients that never meet each other. Think about portions, storage and the amount of cooking you want to do that evening.

If you’re feeding several people, ask about quantities rather than guessing from the display. Tell the counter staff whether an item is the main part of a meal or one small part of a larger spread. That information can make the recommendation more useful.

Going with friends who eat differently

A group food outing works better when people can make their own choices. Don’t assume everyone wants to split the same sandwich, spend the same amount or eat the same ingredients. A quick conversation before ordering can prevent a surprisingly complicated bill afterward.

For allergies or dietary restrictions, ask the specific business preparing the food. A general description of a market doesn’t answer questions about ingredients, shared equipment or substitutions. Be clear about what you need and give staff time to respond.

Let the non-food shopper enjoy the neighborhood at their own pace, too. Someone may be interested in the history of the market or simply happy to keep you company. The afternoon doesn’t need everyone to participate in exactly the same way.

Where the Bronx Beer Hall fits

The Bronx Beer Hall is inside the Arthur Avenue Retail Market. For some visitors it offers a place to make the outing more social; for others, the food counters are enough of a destination on their own.

If you want to include the Beer Hall, confirm its current hours and arrangements separately from the businesses around it. One address can contain several operations with different schedules. Plan a way home that doesn’t depend on driving after drinking.

Save room for a return visit

Arthur Avenue is easier to enjoy when you don’t treat the first visit as your only chance. Pick one thing to try now and keep another idea for later. Returning with a clearer sense of the area can be more satisfying than trying to finish a checklist in a single afternoon.

You might make one trip about ingredients and another about lunch. You might come back with someone who knows the neighborhood well, or revisit a counter after learning how you liked the item at home. Those small follow-ups are how a place becomes familiar.

This is also why a food guide doesn’t need to announce that it has discovered a neighborhood. The businesses were here doing their work before your first visit, and the pleasure is in getting to know them.

The practical finish

Use 2344 Arthur Avenue for the Retail Market, confirm individual business hours and leave time for an unhurried look around. Bring a suitable bag, decide how perishables will get home and ask ingredient questions directly at the counter.

Most of all, let your appetite and your actual day set the pace. A good market afternoon can be lunch and a conversation, a bag of dinner ingredients or the beginning of a new routine. It doesn’t have to become a performance to be worth the trip.
